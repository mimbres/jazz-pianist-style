Where the style lives, two ways

The paper's companion page asks its pianist classifier where in a performance it is most and least sure of the pianist. This page asks the paper's conditioned generator the same question: at each moment, how much better does the true pianist's label predict the notes than the strongest rival label? Both curves use the same 1024-token windows, the same within-track z-score and the same smoothing, so they can be laid on top of each other. How the two curves are made

Classifier (paper, Section 7): the Aria-based pianist classifier reads each 1024-token window (about 300 notes) and we take its logit for the true pianist minus its strongest other logit. Windows advance 128 tokens, so neighbours overlap by 87%. The margins are z-scored within the track and smoothed with a Gaussian of 1.5 windows.

Likelihood (this experiment): the paper's cross-attention generator scores the same windows under every artist label with teacher forcing. The margin is Σ log p(token | true pianist) − max over rivals of Σ log p(token | rival), then the identical z-score and smoothing. The faint trace and the second piano roll use the per-token evidence against the track's runner-up label, smoothed over about ten notes, a resolution the window method cannot reach.

The generator scores 4096-token chunks independently, so its context resets at the dashed lines.
